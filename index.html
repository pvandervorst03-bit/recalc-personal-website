01 / A LITTLE BACKGROUND

Three places.
One through-line.

I grew up in Naperville, Illinois, where Scouting, distance running, orchestra, and a youth business fair taught me that the best teams combine individual preparation with shared responsibility.

At the University of South Carolina, I found finance: a field where numbers matter, but judgment matters more. Since then, I have tried to get as close to the work as possible—from sourcing lower-middle-market businesses to researching technology companies.

A summer in Dordrecht, the Netherlands, made the work more global and more concrete. Building ROIC trees for portfolio companies showed me that “value creation” is really a series of decisions made by real operators.

The thread is simple: enter unfamiliar rooms, listen closely, earn responsibility, and leave something useful behind.

HOW I’LL SHOW UP

Useful from day one

I know that what I get out of Recalc will depend on what I put into it. I want to be the person who signs up for the extra mock, follows up after an introduction, shares an opportunity when I find one, and stays involved after the seven sessions are over.

I’ve learned that I enjoy taking what I figure out and making it useful for the next person, whether that meant building a ten-week ETA curriculum at ExecCap or helping create a fair for 400 young entrepreneurs. I would bring that same habit to Recalc.

The last two years have taught me that I like being useful to a group more than I like trying to impress one. Recalc feels like the next version of that: a few hundred people trying to break into the same rooms, helping each other get there.

THE QUESTION I’M CARRYING

How do you recognize a great business before the answer looks obvious?

I don’t expect one formula. I want better frameworks, more repetitions, people who challenge my assumptions, and enough humility to keep changing my mind.
